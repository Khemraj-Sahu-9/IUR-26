## OPENING

Welcome. Today, we walk through the ASHA Worker Digital Platform — a system we built to digitize the daily field operations of India's community health workers.

ASHA stands for Accredited Social Health Activist. These are frontline workers who travel to remote villages, visit households, track pregnant women, monitor newborns, distribute medicines, and create referrals to Primary Health Centres. [PAUSE]

The problem is simple and severe. Most of this work still happens on paper. Paper registers get lost. Data gets duplicated. Supply requests get delayed. And critically — in the very areas where these workers operate, internet connectivity is unreliable or non-existent.

Our platform addresses this directly. It is a Progressive Web App, or PWA, that allows an ASHA worker to perform all of her digital work — recording visits, creating follow-ups, requesting medicines — even when she has no internet connection. When connectivity returns, the system synchronizes her records automatically and securely.

---

## WHAT THE PLATFORM DOES

`[Show Login Screen]`

At its core, the platform is a multi-role healthcare application. It supports three distinct roles: the ASHA field worker, the Sector Supervisor, and the PHC Manager. Each role sees only the interface and data relevant to their responsibilities.

The platform covers household registration, patient management, clinical visit recording, follow-up scheduling, referral creation, pregnancy tracking, child health monitoring, medicine inventory, medicine request workflows, task management, notifications, operational reporting, and a bilingual interface supporting English and Hindi.

All of these features are implemented and functional in the current codebase.

## STARTING THE APPLICATION

`[Show Login Screen]`

The application opens to a login screen. The user can sign in with email and password, or — for demonstration purposes — use one of three pre-configured personas that log in with a single tap. [PAUSE]

There is Sunita Devi, an ASHA field worker. Dr. Anita Roy, a Sector Supervisor. And Rajesh Sharma, a PHC Manager.

The language can be switched between English and Hindi directly on the login screen, before authentication. This preference is stored locally and persists across sessions.

Once authenticated, the system retrieves the user's profile from the database, identifies their role, and loads the corresponding portal. An ASHA sees the Field Worker Portal. A Supervisor sees the Sector Supervision Portal. A Manager sees the PHC Administration Portal. There is no way for a user to access another role's interface.

## THE ASHA DASHBOARD

`[Open ASHA Dashboard]`

The ASHA dashboard is designed as a mobile-first operational control centre. At the top, a personalized greeting shows the worker's name and her assigned ward. A notification bell displays unread alerts with a live counter.

Below that, four summary cards show real, database-driven metrics: today's recorded visits, pending follow-ups, pending medicine refill requests, and patients requiring attention. These numbers come directly from the data — they are not placeholders.

Next, four navigation tiles provide quick access to Households, Patients, Tasks and Visits, and the Drug Kit. Each tile shows a live count from the database. There are also quick-action buttons to add a new household or register a new patient, plus a link to the ASHA's personal work report.

At the bottom of the screen, a persistent navigation bar offers five tabs: Home, Households, Patients, Tasks, and Profile.

## HOUSEHOLD AND PATIENT WORKFLOW

`[Open Households List]`

The ASHA begins her work by managing households. She can view all her assigned households, search by name, and add new ones. Each household records the head of family, address, village, and ward.

`[Open Household Detail → Open a Patient]`

Inside a household, the worker can see all registered family members and add new patients. Each patient record includes their full name, date of birth, gender, phone number, relationship to the head of household, and a unique patient code generated by the system.

`[Open Patient Profile]`

The patient profile is the central hub. From here, the ASHA can see the patient's demographic information, view their visit history, check pending follow-ups, review referrals, and — for female patients — access pregnancy tracking. For children, a dedicated child tracking section appears. The profile also provides action buttons to record a new visit, create a referral, or edit the patient's information.

## FIELD VISIT AND FOLLOW-UP

`[Show Add Visit Screen]`

To record a visit, the ASHA selects the visit type — options include Routine ANC, Post-Natal Care, Immunization, General Checkup, Maternal Checkup, Child Growth, or Communicable Disease. She enters the visit date, any clinical notes, and optionally marks that a follow-up is required. If she does, the system asks for a follow-up date and notes, and automatically creates a scheduled follow-up record.

Follow-ups appear in the ASHA's task list organized by status: today, upcoming, overdue, and completed. She can mark a follow-up as completed when the patient has been seen.

---

## REFERRAL WORKFLOW

`[Show Add Referral Screen]`

When a patient needs care beyond what the ASHA can provide, she creates a referral. The form captures the destination — such as a sub-centre, PHC, or district hospital — the reason for referral, the referral date, and any notes. Referral status progresses through stages: referred, visited, admitted, discharged, or cancelled. The supervisor can monitor these referrals from their portal.

---

## MATERNAL AND CHILD TRACKING

The platform includes dedicated tracking for maternal and child health. For female patients, the ASHA can register a pregnancy with the date of last menstrual period, expected due date, gravida, and para values. The system calculates the gestational age automatically. Supervisors see all active pregnancies across their sector on a dedicated Maternal tab.

For children, the system tracks immunizations and growth visits through the child tracking section within the patient profile.

---

## MEDICINE WORKFLOW

`[Open Drug Kit View]`

The medicine workflow is a complete supply chain loop. The ASHA views her available drug kit, sees current stock levels, and submits a request for a specific medicine with a quantity. This request enters a queue with a status of pending.

The Supervisor reviews the request on their Medicines tab and can approve or reject it. The PHC Manager then sees approved requests on their Requisitions tab, manages central stock inventory, and marks orders as fulfilled. Throughout this process, notifications are generated for relevant parties. [PAUSE]

Medicine stock includes minimum quantity thresholds, and the Manager's dashboard highlights low-stock and out-of-stock items.

The overview shows sector-level statistics — active ASHAs, weekly visit counts, pending follow-ups, and active referrals. The monitoring tab displays recent field visits and open referrals. ## OFFLINE-FIRST ARCHITECTURE

`[EMPHASIZE]`

Now we come to what is arguably the most important technical feature of this platform: offline-first operation. [PAUSE]

`[Turn Internet OFF]`

When the ASHA worker enters an area with no internet connectivity, the application continues to work. She can create new households, register patients, record visits, create follow-ups, submit referrals, and request medicines — all completely offline.

This is possible because the application uses a technology called IndexedDB — specifically through a library called Dexie.js — which provides structured local storage directly inside the browser. The application maintains thirteen local data stores that mirror the server database. Every time the user creates or modifies a record, the data is immediately saved to this local store, and the change is added to a synchronization queue.

`[Turn Internet ON]`

When connectivity returns, the synchronization engine activates automatically. It processes the queue in a carefully designed order: households first, then patients, then pregnancies, then visits, follow-ups, referrals, and medicine orders. This ordering ensures that parent records — like the household a patient belongs to — are synchronized to the server before their dependent child records.

Each record uses a universally unique identifier generated on the client device. When the system sends a record to the server, it uses an upsert operation with conflict detection on this identifier. This means that even if the network drops during synchronization and the same record is sent twice, it will not create a duplicate. The operation is what engineers call idempotent — it can be safely replayed without side effects.

If any individual record fails to synchronize, it is retained in the queue and retried up to five times. Failed records never cause data loss. They remain safely on the device.

## SECURITY

Security is enforced at multiple layers. Authentication uses JSON Web Tokens through Supabase Auth. The database itself enforces twenty-six Row Level Security policies — often abbreviated as RLS. This means that even if someone attempts to bypass the application's user interface, the database will reject any unauthorized data access. An ASHA worker can only see her own assigned households and patients. A Supervisor sees only their sector's data.

All critical actions — creating visits, completing follow-ups, approving medicine requests — are recorded in an audit log that captures who performed the action, what was changed, and when.

---

## REPORTING

Each role has access to relevant operational reports. The ASHA sees her personal visit summary, follow-up completion rates, referral status, and medicine order history. The Supervisor sees aggregated sector metrics including visit distribution by type and active pregnancy counts. The Manager sees stock level reports and supply chain fulfillment metrics. Reports support configurable date ranges: today, last seven days, last thirty days, this month, or a custom range.

---

## TECHNICAL STACK

The frontend is built with React and TypeScript, compiled with Vite, and styled with Tailwind CSS. The PWA engine uses Workbox through the vite-plugin-pwa package for service worker caching, and Dexie.js version four for IndexedDB storage. The backend runs on Supabase, which provides PostgreSQL seventeen as the database, authentication, and real-time capabilities. The application includes eighty-four automated end-to-end tests built with Playwright.

## END-TO-END STORY

`[SHOW]`

Let me bring this all together with one complete scenario. [PAUSE]

Sunita Devi, our ASHA worker, logs into the application on her phone. Her dashboard shows three pending follow-ups and one medicine refill request. She taps into Households, opens the Sharma family, and selects Priya Sharma's profile. She records a routine ANC visit and schedules a follow-up for next week. She also notices that Priya needs a referral to the district hospital, so she creates one.

Then Sunita walks to a remote village with no mobile signal. She continues her work — registering a new family, adding two patients, and recording visits — all offline. Her phone stores everything locally.

Two hours later, she returns to an area with coverage. The application detects the network, and synchronization begins. Households sync first, then patients, then visits. Every record uploads successfully. No duplicates. No errors.

Meanwhile, Dr. Anita Roy, the Sector Supervisor, opens her portal and sees the new referral, the updated visit counts, and the active pregnancy with its gestational age. She also sees a medicine request from Sunita and approves it.

Rajesh Sharma, the PHC Manager, sees the approved request, checks his central stock levels, and fulfills the order. Sunita receives a notification.

`[Show Notification]`

This is the complete cycle. From the field, through administration, back to the field — digitized and reliable, whether the internet is available or not.

## LIMITATIONS

To be transparent about the current state: the "Forgot Password" feature on the login screen provides guidance rather than sending an actual password reset email. Ward and sector names displayed in the dashboard headers are static text rather than dynamically assigned. Pregnancy tracking uses a localStorage-based fallback in addition to the database when operating offline. These are known areas for future refinement.
